# Paper Outline and Evidence Map

**Status:** Draft for author review; outline only, not a manuscript.

## Working configuration (provisional)

- Paper type: empirical research article, IMRaD.
- Body language: English.
- Abstract: English; add Simplified Chinese only if required by the target venue.
- Citation style: pending target venue.
- Target journal and quartile system/year: pending author/institution confirmation. “SCI Q2” is a planning target, not a verified venue classification.
- Working title: **Training-Consistent Pressure-Prior Integration for Independent Multi-Agent Traffic Signal Control: Confirmatory Evidence and Topology-Dependent Boundaries**.
- Main claim: Under the pre-registered evaluation scope, training-consistent integration of a pressure-based action prior (IPPO-P) improved demand service time relative to IPPO. Exploratory 3x3 results show that the gain is not universal and may reverse at some topology-load settings.

## Research question and hypotheses

**RQ:** Does incorporating a pressure-based action prior consistently into both rollout action selection and PPO updates improve demand service time over standard IPPO under the tested multi-intersection traffic scenarios, and how does the observed effect vary in exploratory 3x3 settings?

**Confirmatory H1:** Across H1, H2, and H3 with equal scenario weights, IPPO-P reduces demand service time relative to IPPO over ten new independent training seeds.

**Exploratory questions:**
- How do the scenario-specific effects vary across H1/H2/H3?
- Do the 3x3 scale-up runs show that the confirmatory result generalizes across network topology and demand?
- Is the observed performance pattern consistent with a variance-stabilization account? This remains a hypothesis, not a tested causal mechanism.

## Proposed manuscript structure

### 1. Introduction
1. Traffic signal control as a multi-agent sequential decision problem; coordination and non-stationarity motivate robust policy learning.
2. Independent PPO is a useful decentralized baseline, but local policy updates do not explicitly encode traffic-pressure preferences.
3. Define the narrow intervention: add a normalized pressure-derived action prior consistently to the policy logits during both rollout sampling and PPO likelihood evaluation/update.
4. State the gap conservatively: this study evaluates a training-consistent prior integration against IPPO under a frozen multi-scenario protocol; it does not claim a new general traffic-pressure principle or universal superiority.
5. Contributions, limited to what the data establish:
   - a precisely specified and reproducible IPPO-P implementation with rollout/update distribution consistency;
   - a pre-registered, seed-level confirmatory comparison over three traffic scenarios;
   - an explicit report of topology/load boundary evidence, including negative and underpowered exploratory results.
6. End with hypotheses and paper organization.

### 2. Related Work
2.1 Reinforcement learning for traffic signal control.
2.2 Independent and multi-agent PPO for decentralized traffic control.
2.3 Pressure-based traffic control and pressure-informed policies.
2.4 Positioning: distinguish the present work from pressure-based reward design, classical max-pressure control, graph communication, and post-hoc action biasing. An initial search found adjacent pressure-aware RL, including PDLight's PRCOL reward/action logic, multi-hop upstream pressure RL, and a recent TSC reinforcement-learning review. This is enough to proceed with a cautious empirical paper outline, but not enough to substantiate novelty or complete Related Work. Verify bibliographic metadata and compare full methods before making any priority claim.

### 3. Method
3.1 Problem formulation: intersections as agents, observation/action spaces, SUMO transition process, and objective.
3.2 IPPO baseline and shared experimental controls.
3.3 Pressure prior construction:
- Let raw phase/action pressure scores be centered within each intersection and scaled by within-intersection standard deviation with a floor of 1.
- Clip normalized scores to [-3, 3].
- Define IPPO-P logits as baseline actor logits plus 2.25 times the normalized prior.
- No hold bonus; policy temperature 1.0.
3.4 Training-consistent PPO likelihood:
- The same observation, prior vector, temperature, sampled action, and old log-probability are used in rollout and update.
- Explain why changing only evaluation logits would not be the same treatment and would invalidate the PPO importance ratio semantics.
3.5 Training and evaluation protocol: 50 episodes; evaluation seeds 1042–1046; scenario details in Table 1; training seeds 47–56 for primary confirmation.
3.6 Outcomes: primary demand_service_time U (lower is better), computed over all planned demand vehicles as mean max(0, service_end − scheduled_departure); normal arrivals use arrival time capped at demand_end, while all other planned vehicles are censored at demand_end. It is therefore not the mean travel time among completed vehicles. Secondary metrics: completed-vehicle travel time and delay; lane-aggregated queue, waiting, and speed; planned_completion_rate = normal compliant arrivals / planned demand; completion_rate = completed / departed. Report the two completion denominators separately. Queue is mean halting count × 7.5 m over unique controlled lanes; waiting is mean accumulated waiting over unique vehicles on those lanes; speed is vehicle-weighted mean over the lane union.

### 4. Experimental Design and Statistical Analysis
4.1 Pre-registration and separation of pilot from confirmation:
- Protocol frozen 2026-10-24 before the new data.
- Pilot seeds 42–46 excluded from primary inference.
- Primary data use new training seeds 47–56 only.
4.2 Paired design and estimand:
- Within each training seed, average U over five fixed evaluation seeds.
- For each seed, equally average log(U_IPPO/U_IPPO-P) over H1/H2/H3.
- Transform the mean log ratio to percent improvement as 100 × [1 − exp(−mean log ratio)].
4.3 Primary hypothesis test: exact two-sided paired sign-flip test, alpha .05, one pre-registered primary contrast, no multiplicity correction for the primary test.
4.4 Uncertainty: 20,000 paired bootstrap resamples over training seeds, seed 20261024; interval is descriptive and does not replace the exact primary test.
4.5 Secondary analyses: scenario-specific effects, pilot-plus-confirmation pooled 15-seed descriptive summary only, completion/teleport/conservation checks.
4.6 Exploratory extensions: H1HIGH (2700 veh/h, n=5), H1SAT (5400 veh/h, n=5), and H1PROBE700 (6300 veh/h, n=2). Explicitly state these were post-confirmation exploratory extensions; do not merge into the primary test or portray as confirmatory.
4.7 Reproducibility: task manifests retain source SHA-256 values, SUMO/environment settings, route files, method settings, and checkpoint paths. Training uses the checkpoint written by the 50-episode training run and reloads it for deterministic evaluation in fresh environments. Git commit, runtime/library versions, hardware, and checkpoint-file hashes are not captured in the frozen manifest and must be recovered from archives or disclosed as unavailable.

### 5. Results
5.1 Protocol completion and integrity checks: 60/60 planned tasks completed; across 300 evaluation episodes, demand conservation passed in all 300, with zero teleports. Planned completion ranged from 98.681% to 99.056%. In these runs completion_rate and planned_completion_rate numerically match, but retain their distinct denominators. These 300 episodes are repeated evaluations nested within training tasks, not independent training replicates.
5.2 Primary confirmatory result (Table 2; Figure 2): IPPO-P vs IPPO, +1.712%, bootstrap 95% CI [1.252%, 2.202%], 10/10 seed effects positive, exact two-sided sign-flip p=.001953.
5.3 Scenario-specific secondary results (Table 3): H1 +0.407% (8/10), H2 +1.023% (10/10), H3 +3.675% (10/10); keep explicitly secondary and do not imply three independent confirmatory tests.
5.4 Exploratory 3x3 scale-up and boundary results (Figure 3/Table 4):
- H1HIGH, 2700 veh/h: +0.458%, 5/5 positive, n=5 exact p=.0625; descriptive only.
- H1SAT, 5400 veh/h: -0.997%, 0/5 positive, n=5 exact p=.0625; report global service-quality degradation and completion/teleport audits, without claiming a causal mechanism.
- H1PROBE700, 6300 veh/h: paired U improves in 2/2 seeds, but one effect is only 0.027%; descriptive probe only, not confirmation.
- These 3x3 flow totals were verified from 18 route-flow entries: 18 × 150 = 2700 veh/h, 18 × 300 = 5400 veh/h, and 18 × 350 = 6300 veh/h. Assuming the nine signalized intersections as the denominator, totals correspond to nominal 300, 600, and 700 veh/h per intersection, respectively. Route-file comments contain inconsistent per-intersection figures; report counts and totals from the flow entries and do not treat labels such as “saturated” as independent evidence of network saturation. Also avoid describing the 3x3 route set as balanced across all approaches: the 18 flows comprise 12 through routes and 6 turns, not an equal route allocation by movement class.
- Do not draw a smooth or monotonic pressure-response curve; topology, routes, load and sample size differ.
5.5 Pilot four-grid GC results (Supplementary Table or clearly labeled pilot subsection): report as exploratory architecture context only. Do not infer graph-mechanism contribution or equivalence from non-significance.

### 6. Discussion
6.1 Main finding: a modest but consistent confirmatory improvement in the pre-registered scope.
6.2 Practical interpretation: the treatment is the complete training-consistent prior integration, not an isolated inference-time bias.
6.3 Boundary evidence: observed gains do not establish universal improvement; the 3x3 results vary by demand/topology and include a consistent negative point.
6.4 Mechanism: pressure-prior stabilization is a plausible hypothesis only. The available data do not contain intersection-level pressure/action/switch trajectories needed to identify causal mechanisms.
6.5 Relation to prior work: interpret only after verified literature comparison.
6.6 Limitations:
- only one simulator family and synthetic grid configurations;
- modest number of training seeds (10 confirmatory; exploratory extensions n=5 and n=2);
- topology and demand are not factorially separated in the 3x3 extensions;
- no intersection-level diagnostic traces for the 5400/6300 veh/h runs;
- pilot results informed method selection and must remain separate from confirmatory inference;
- no evidence of universal superiority, real-world deployment, or safety guarantees.
6.7 Implications for future work: factorial topology-by-load experiments and local action/pressure trace logging, if pursued as future work rather than adding more post hoc experiments here.

### 7. Conclusion
Conclude only that IPPO-P improved the primary metric under the frozen H1/H2/H3 protocol and that exploratory 3x3 evidence cautions against universal extrapolation. Do not claim a proven pressure threshold, variance-stabilization mechanism, or general traffic-control superiority.
